This paper investigates whether conditional volatility, filtered market-regime probabilities and extreme-loss severity benefit from regime-conditioned entropy weighting in a composite equity-market stress indicator. The Adaptive Composite Market Stress Index (A-CMSI) combines ARIMA-conditional-variance models, forward-filtered Gaussian hidden Markov probabilities and rolling peaks-over-threshold expected shortfall for AAPL, MSFT and SPY. Fresh estimation reproduces the previously reported five-state results on 2,765 adjusted-price observations from 2015-2025 and 1,810 scored 30-day forecasts: Adaptive RMSE is 0.10532, compared with 0.10457 for Equal and 0.10411 for NoHMM. This revision executes complete three-, four-, five- and six-state paths, independently removes panic probability and regime multipliers, evaluates direct and deficit entropy weights, and tests nine EVT window/threshold configurations. The controlled Adaptive specification has RMSE 0.10532 on 1810 matched dates; the lowest point-estimate RMSE among the displayed composite/baseline variants is obtained by NoHMM. Five EVT configurations cannot meet the fixed minimum-exceedance rule and become entirely empirical fallback estimates. The study separates after-close detection from future-event prediction, reports dependence-aware uncertainty and multiplicity-adjusted loss comparisons, and retains unfavorable baselines. Adaptive weighting has no demonstrated predictive advantage over simpler weighting baselines in this sample. The evidence supports a transparent research framework, not a deployment-ready systemic-risk indicator.
